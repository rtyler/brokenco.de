I have spent the last number of years trying to tune the solar and home battery
set up to ensure that I am giving PG&E as little money as possible. Seriously,
that company sucks out loud. Anyways, winter is a bit challenging as my the arc
of the sun no longer fully charges the battery during the day _and_ the
increased load from electric-driven heaters pushes my overall power usage out
of "surplus" into "consumption."

Most of the day the battery is in "self-consumption" mode. The house runs off
whatever is available, solar first and then the battery, and the grid only
fills in what is left. Nothing clever is happening there but I am trying to
optimize for the cheapest power which is whatever I am pulling from the roof.

Two hours before the utility's "partial peak" window starts, the battery
switches to charge from the grid. By the time partial peak and then peak arrive
the battery is full and the house draws from it instead of from the grid at the
most expensive hours of the day.

The first winter season with the battery I was loathe to charge from the grid
because it just _felt_ wasteful. The math however maths in your favor for
charging "off-peak".

PG&E's EV2-A rate, roughly speaking:

- Off-peak, midnight to 3 p.m.: $0.22558/kWh
- Partial-peak, 3 to 4 p.m. and 9 p.m. to midnight: $0.39428/kWh
- Peak, 4 to 9 p.m.: $0.41099/kWh

Those are the bundled winter rates from [Electric Schedule
EV2](https://www.pge.com/tariffs/assets/pdf/tariffbook/ELEC_SCHEDS_EV2%20(Sch).pdf),
effective June 1, 2026.

The **peak** power is almost 2x as expensive as the off-peak at _any point_
between midnight and 3pm. The mental block I had to "just charge off the grid"
was a misplaced optimism that I might get enough sunlight to make that
_wasteful_ since [the cost of power](/2021/07/25/cost-of-power.html) is so
asymmetrical in the utility's favor. In short, if _any_ excesss produced the
utility will pay you less than 35% of what they would charge you for the same
kilowatt hour.

The summer excess solar production starts as early as 1pm most days, which is
fantastic.

In winter however I have to be realistic. I will *never* have excess solar. The
sun is too low in the sky, the days are too short, it just won't happen.
Charging from the grid for 15kWh offpeak, guarantees that I don't use any
partial or on-peak power. 

The winter math finally maths!

I am starting to ponder whether more solar panels or an additional grid-charged
battery would be more efficient in winter..

The compulsion to optimize is enhanced by the desire to "stick it to PG&E",
probably the most hated company in California, and that's saying something!
